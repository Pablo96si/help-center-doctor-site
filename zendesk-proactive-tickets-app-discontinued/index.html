Zendesk is discontinuing the Proactive Tickets app on 21 October 2026: what to use instead

If your team uses the Proactive Tickets app from the Zendesk Marketplace to e-mail groups of customers, it stops working on 21 October 2026. This page explains what changes, what does not, the options for after that date with their prices as listed, and a plan to move in 20 minutes without losing a campaign.

34 days left, counted on 17 September 2026.

What is happening

On 7 April 2026 Zendesk announced the discontinuation of seven Built-by-Zendesk apps, among them Proactive Tickets, the free Marketplace app that let agents create a ticket for each user in a list and e-mail them in one go. The timeline in the announcement: no longer available for download since 7 April 2026, no longer supported after 30 June 2026, and it stops functioning after 21 October 2026. Zendesk's stated reason is low usage and capabilities moving into the Agent Workspace; its suggested replacement is the bulk-create tickets API or a custom app built on it (Zendesk published an App Builder recipe on 24 April 2026 with the note that it cannot guarantee the recipe keeps working over time), and it notes that Support has no native bulk-ticketing tool. This guide is written by an independent developer and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Zendesk; the announcement is the authoritative source for dates.

What stays and what stops

  • Tickets already created stay. They are ordinary tickets in your account, with their tags, comments and replies. Views, triggers and reports that use them keep working.
  • The triggers stay. The standard "Notify requester of new proactive ticket" trigger is part of Zendesk, not of the app. Any tool that creates a public ticket on a user's behalf can keep using it.
  • The app's own screens stop. Saved audiences, message drafts and campaign lists inside the discontinued app are gone once it is removed. Copy what you need before 21 October: the audiences you use most (tags, organizations, saved searches) and the messages you send regularly.
  • Nothing changes in the API. Bulk ticket creation, search and job statuses are unchanged; that is what every replacement, including ours, is built on.

How to choose in one minute

  • Choose GrowthDot if you run marketing-style campaigns where open and click tracking, scheduling and A/B tests matter more than keeping data inside your own account, and your team is small enough for per-agent pricing.
  • Choose Sparkly if you want a vendor-hosted tool priced per account and its monthly caps match your volume.
  • Write your own if you have engineers, a low volume and time to get exclusions, duplicates after a crash, rate limits and opt-outs right.
  • Choose Proactive Outreach if you want the tickets created from inside Support with your own session, nothing leaving the account, a free plan that covers the long tail the discontinued app served, and a report that counts replies and opt-outs.
  • Do nothing if you sent a campaign once a year. Keep the ticket history; when the next incident comes, pick one of the options above.

Move in 20 minutes

The full walkthrough with screenshots is on its own page: Migrate from the Proactive Tickets app to Proactive Outreach in 20 minutes. The short version:

  1. Copy your audiences and messages

    While the old app still opens, write down the tags, organizations and searches you use and paste your regular messages into a document. Three minutes.

  2. Check the delivery setup

    Proactive Outreach checks that the notification trigger is active and creates a tag-scoped one if it is missing, plus the STOP-reply trigger, each with one click from Delivery setup.

  3. Decide who may send

    A campaign to thousands of customers should not be one click away for every agent. Restrict sending to a group and set the opt-out tag.

  4. Run a five-person test

    Send a test ticket to yourself, then a campaign of five colleagues: check the sender address, the subject, the placeholders, the ticket status and where replies land.

  5. Send the first real one small

    Under 100 people. Read the report, then scale.

Questions

Will customers notice a difference? No. A proactive ticket is a ticket created on the customer's behalf with a public comment; the e-mail they receive comes from your trigger and your support address, whichever tool created the ticket.

Can I import my old campaigns? The discontinued app has no export. The tickets it created remain searchable, for example by the tags you used; Proactive Outreach can build an audience from the requesters of those tickets, which is often what "send it again" means.

Can I keep using the app after 21 October? Zendesk says it stops functioning after that date. Plan the move before, while the app still opens and you can copy your audiences out.
